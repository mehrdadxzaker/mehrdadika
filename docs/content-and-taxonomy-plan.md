# Persian Word Atlas Content and Taxonomy Plan

This is the proposed content design for an open-source English vocabulary learning website for Persian speakers. The website should preserve Word Atlas's visual browsing, word cards, listening, recall, review, and progress experience while supporting a comprehensive subject catalog. Content will be published in reviewed modules over time.

The complete category map belongs in the initial project design. A category's presence in that map does not imply that its lessons have been published. Learners should always be able to distinguish available material from planned coverage.

### Cooking coverage

Cooking has its own domain, with beginner everyday language and specialist culinary vocabulary. Its modules cover ingredients, preparation verbs, methods, utensils/appliances, baking/pastry, beverages, flavors/textures, recipes and measurements, cuisines, dining/service, storage, hygiene and allergen terminology. Examples include `whisk`, `simmer`, `knead`, `fold in`, `pinch`, and `preheat`. Recipe excerpts, menus, kitchen scenes, technique diagrams and ingredient/utensil identification use the existing cards, readers and practice modes. The examples are proposed headwords, not reviewed published entries.

A discovery group is a browsing aid; it is not another tag that creates independent copies. Shared concepts reference one sense ID. Moving an existing subcategory to its dedicated family preserves its ID and keeps links from the previous discovery route. New siblings require a coverage rationale and an overlap check, while new subcategories can be added without changes to the five-tab shell.

Every subcategory has one canonical parent for navigation. Cross-domain relationships allow, for example, anatomy to appear in medicine and biology, and building structures in engineering and architecture. A cross-link must not create another content copy or another review obligation for the same sense.

Taxonomy changes should be reviewed through ordinary pull requests. Renaming a label preserves its ID. Moving a subcategory preserves its ID and adds a route redirect where needed. Retired IDs remain in a migration registry.

## 2. Independent dimensions

Keep these dimensions separate in data and navigation:

| Dimension | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Domain | Organize the subject | Medicine → clinical communication |
| Learning path | Order material toward a goal | Understanding a nursing handover |
| English level | Estimate language difficulty | Intermediate wording and sentence structure |
| Domain depth | Express required subject knowledge | Introductory anatomy versus specialist terminology |
| Learner role | Tailor context | Student, researcher, nurse, technician |
| Language function | Group recurring communication | Describing a process, comparing results, explaining a fault |
| Content type | Choose presentation and practice | Word, phrase, abbreviation, diagram, process |
| Register | Explain context of use | Everyday, patient-facing, professional, academic |

English level must not be inferred from the difficulty of a medical or engineering concept. CEFR labels for specialist vocabulary should remain unset where the project has no defensible assignment. An English definition can be simple even when its concept is advanced.

Learning paths reference published senses from multiple categories. Shared academic language, quantities, cause and effect, and professional communication can therefore serve many paths. The initial experience should use Word Atlas's familiar category filters and daily-learning flow; paths can be exposed as an additional selector without a new competing navigation system.

## 3. Vocabulary identity and learning state

Use four distinct units:

1. **Lexical item:** a word or phrase with a stable `lexicalItemId`, headword, and pronunciation variants. Different parts of speech and meanings remain under that lexical item where they share the same headword.
2. **Sense:** one meaning in a defined context, with its own stable `senseId`, part of speech, Persian explanation, examples, domain links, and review status.
3. **Public word card:** one browsable card and one galaxy star per lexical item, preserving Word Atlas's word-centered presentation. The card contains its relevant published senses rather than multiplying the same headword into separate public cards.
4. **Practice prompt:** an exercise targeting a particular published sense, using the existing Word Atlas learning loop and supported question formats. Scheduling remains independent per sense underneath the shared public card.

For example, the noun `discharge` can have a patient-release sense and an electrical sense. Learning either sense does not establish mastery of the other. A sense appearing in multiple categories or paths refers to one learner memory record, avoiding duplicate scheduled reviews.

A category or path defines a scope over published senses. Its word grid and galaxy deduplicate their lexical item IDs, while opening a word card emphasizes the meanings relevant to that scope. Other published meanings may be available through a clearly labeled expansion. An all-category view includes every published sense for that lexical item.

A star's status aggregates only the published senses in the active scope. Use `unseen` when none has been introduced, `learning` while the senses are being learned without a familiar subset, `partial` when some scoped senses are familiar and others are not, and `familiar` only when every scoped sense meets the defined scheduling threshold. A single correct answer cannot establish familiarity for untested meanings. Show a meaning-level breakdown such as “1 of 3 published meanings familiar,” and label the scope so a familiar medical sense cannot imply familiarity with the electrical sense.

Multiword expressions such as `within tolerance` are first-class lexical items. Abbreviations connect to their expanded forms, with domain-specific meanings separated. Related words, synonyms, antonyms, prerequisites, and easily confused concepts are typed relationships rather than undifferentiated links.

Persist progress using stable sense IDs, with a migration adapter if upstream progress is keyed by headword. Small editorial corrections preserve progress. Splitting or materially changing a meaning requires an explicit migration, retaining previous history and identifying any newly introduced learning unit. Removing a card from publication must not delete its learner history.

## 4. Content record

Canonical source records should be readable JSON or YAML in version control, validated by a schema. A build adapter can emit the compact indexes and entry chunks required by the upstream application. Avoid coupling editorial metadata to the runtime card layout.

Full records should additionally support Persian alternate equivalents, common loanwords, usage notes, confusables, sentence audio, media descriptions, source locators, review timestamps, and a revision history. Do not invent a second example or a recording merely to satisfy a card template; support an explicit unavailable state until a validated asset exists.

Persian search should normalize Arabic/Persian letter variants and optional spacing for matching while preserving the editorial spelling on screen. Store alternate terms as searchable aliases. Establish an Iranian Persian terminology baseline for the first release, with explicit regional labels for any later Dari or other variants; the product should not silently treat every Persian usage as interchangeable.

### Reading content

Support both rights-cleared short passages and longer books divided into chapters. Reading should reuse the published vocabulary catalog: an English word or phrase in a passage opens its existing public word card, with the linked meaning emphasized. Reading selections remain coherent passages with comprehension activities and their own resume state.

The minimal model is:

| Record | Required fields and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Reading item | Stable `readingItemId`; revision; type `passage` or `book`; EN/FA title; description; author and translator credits; domain and level metadata; source and rights records; review and publication states; ordered chapter IDs where applicable |
| Chapter | Stable `chapterId`; reading item ID; revision; EN/FA title; order; ordered paragraph IDs; review and publication states. A book may publish approved chapters incrementally while showing the availability of remaining chapters. |
| Paragraph | Stable `paragraphId`; chapter or passage parent; ordered sentence IDs; optional structural role such as heading, body, or caption |
| Sentence and alignment | Stable `sentenceId`; English text; Persian text or aligned Persian segment references; alignment group ID where translations have one-to-many or many-to-one sentence correspondence. Preserve reading order for both languages. |
| Vocabulary annotation | Sentence ID; stable annotation ID; checked text-span anchors; `targetSenseIds` pointing to exact published meanings; related lexical item IDs derived from those senses. Selecting a link opens the existing word card. |
| Comprehension item | Stable question ID; linked paragraph or sentence IDs; type; prompt; answer/options as appropriate; explanation; targeted reading objective; optional `targetSenseIds`; review status |
| Narration | Optional reviewed audio asset ID; voice/accent and rights metadata; duration; optional checked sentence or paragraph timing segments |
| Resume state | Learner-local reading item ID and revision; chapter ID; paragraph ID; optional sentence ID and scroll position; last-read timestamp; completion markers and bookmarks |

Missing narration does not block a text-reading release; hide unavailable controls. Enable synchronized highlighting only when reviewed timing segments reference real sentence IDs, have ordered non-overlapping intervals where required by the player, and satisfy `0 <= startMs < endMs <= audioDurationMs`. Text corrections must invalidate affected timing checks until revalidated. Reading progress and comprehension results remain distinct from scheduled vocabulary recall: opening a gloss or completing a chapter does not automatically mark its target senses familiar.

Rights checks cover the original passage or book, Persian translation, illustrations, and narration independently. Public-domain status must be checked for the intended publication context. Contributors may also write original passages. Do not include commercial books, translations, or recordings on the strength of a citation alone.

Passages, chapters, paragraphs, sentences, and question IDs survive ordinary edits. When a revision removes or splits an anchor, a migration should move the bookmark to the nearest retained logical location and preserve the previous record. Reading release validation checks aligned text, annotation spans, target meanings, questions, rights, optional narration, timings, and resume migrations before publication. Draft chapters and unapproved questions stay outside the public reading index.

## 5. Review and publication states

Editorial readiness and public availability are separate fields:

| State dimension | Allowed states | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review | Draft; language review needed; domain review needed; approved; changes requested | Review applies to a specific content revision. Specialist approval cannot be inferred from language approval. |
| Publication | Unpublished; scheduled; published; withdrawn | Only approved revisions can enter a public learning release. |
| Media | Missing; generated or recorded draft; reviewed; published; retired | Each file has its own provenance and rights record. |

General language material needs bilingual editorial approval. Specialist material additionally needs a reviewer competent in the subject. Published terminology should teach language in its context; medical, legal, and financial entries should not expand into individualized professional advice.

Publication is a release operation over a manifest of exact approved revisions and assets. Drafts remain in source control or contributor preview builds, outside the public learning index. A correction to a published entry creates a new revision and passes through the appropriate review gate. Urgent withdrawals use a new release manifest and retain an explanation in the change log.

## 6. Incremental publication and learner behavior

A release module should contain a coherent topic or learning objective, not an arbitrary batch of unrelated generated entries. Its manifest defines included sense revisions, media references, supported exercises, prerequisites, license records, and an ordered learning sequence.

The initial catalog can show all 32 domains in a coverage view. The default study view should emphasize domains with published content. Planned domains may have a clearly labeled informational page and publication roadmap, but no enabled study button or fabricated word count.

Rules for the runtime:

- New-word selection, search results used for practice, category totals, and galaxy stars are derived from the same published manifest. Public cards and stars deduplicate lexical item IDs; scheduling chooses relevant sense IDs within the active category or path.
- A category with no published senses cannot start a session. Explain its availability and offer a published neighboring topic.
- A selected path with insufficient new material can offer global due reviews and other published paths. It must not fill the session with drafts.
- Due reviews remain accessible across categories. A change in the active topic does not strand previously learned material.
- Newly released material does not reset an existing learner's progress or automatically mark them behind a fixed completion percentage.
- Progress labels headword and meaning denominators explicitly, such as “30 of 80 published words explored” and “40 of 120 published meanings familiar,” with a separate indication that the collection is growing. Exploring a card is not a recall outcome.
- Unsupported exercise modes are excluded per item. A missing audio asset must not create an impossible listening question.
- Retired or withdrawn cards preserve history and are excluded from newly scheduled content according to the migration policy.

Coverage pages should report unique published lexical items, unique published senses, modules, reviewed media, reading items and chapters, and supported depth bands. A visible word or headword count deduplicates lexical item IDs; a meaning count deduplicates sense IDs. Cross-category memberships must not inflate the atlas-wide totals. Keep drafted and approved-but-unpublished counts in contributor views unless there is a deliberate public roadmap need.

## 7. Open-source contribution workflow

Recommended repository responsibilities:

```text
content/
  taxonomy/             Domain and subcategory definitions
  entries/              Canonical lexical items and senses
  paths/                Ordered learning paths
  modules/              Coherent publication units
  reading/              Rights-cleared passages, books, chapters and activities
  sources/              References and rights records
  media-manifests/      Asset metadata, hashes, credits and availability
  releases/             Approved publication manifests
docs/content/
  style-guide.md
  persian-terminology.md
  review-policy.md
  contributor-guide.md
  coverage.md
```

Keep these paths adaptable to the upstream repository structure. Their separation represents editorial responsibilities, rather than a requirement to replace existing build tools.

The normal contribution sequence is:

1. Open a topic proposal or select an existing coverage issue. Identify audience, scope, references, dependencies, and potential overlap.
2. Draft entries from permitted sources. Record whether text or media was adapted or AI assisted; preserve upstream attribution where applicable.
3. Run local schema, duplicate-ID, referential-integrity, Persian typography, asset, and release-isolation checks.
4. Submit a pull request with a preview of the exact card and exercise states. Include sources, media rights, changed meanings, and review needs.
5. Obtain bilingual editorial review and specialist review where required. Resolve corrections in the same pull request.
6. Approve the revision. Publication can occur immediately in an approved release or wait for a complete module.
7. Publish a versioned manifest and change log. Verify that study queues, search, galaxy counts, asset loading, and progress migrations agree with it.

Use contribution templates for corrections, new entries, new modules, media replacements, and taxonomy changes. A correction report should identify the sense ID and content revision, not only the English word. Contributor roles should be visible and scoped; a language reviewer is not automatically a clinical or engineering reviewer.

## 8. Licensing, media and provenance

Propose MIT for new application code and CC BY 4.0 for new educational content, matching the upstream separation. Preserve all upstream notices and confirm the exact upstream scope before importing assets. Each third-party font, audio file, image, diagram, and text adaptation retains its actual license record. Do not copy commercial dictionary definitions into the open dataset without permission.

Maintain a source registry with URL or bibliographic identifier, accessed date where relevant, specific passage or entry locator, rights basis, and attribution text. A source citation helps verification but does not itself grant redistribution rights. A public credits page and machine-readable attribution export should be generated from the same records.

Media metadata should include asset ID, hash, file format, dimensions or duration, locale/accent, associated sense IDs, creator or generator, permitted uses, review status, and accessibility text. Prefer diagrams when they explain the meaning; use scenes for everyday language and contrasts or processes for abstract material. Distinguish an accurate technical illustration from a decorative visual.

English word and sentence audio should retain the upstream accent-selection interaction. Persian narration can be a later optional layer. Review specialist pronunciation, abbreviations, diagram labels, and correspondence between audio and the displayed sentence.

Generation tools belong to the contributor workflow. Learners should be able to study the published static material without an AI-provider account or paid generation key. Generation scripts must use explicit timeouts, pooled clients where available, bounded concurrency of at most eight outbound requests per task, at most three retries with backoff and jitter, cancellation, and cleanup. Default to a smaller batch for expensive media generation.

## 9. Publication sequence and acceptance gates

| Stage | Content deliverable | Acceptance gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | All 32 domain definitions; shared schema; Persian style guide; source and license policy; empty-state behavior | Every domain is addressable; unpublished material cannot enter any public study selector |
| Reference module | One reviewed module exercising ordinary words, a phrase, multiple senses, audio, and meaningful imagery | The entire upstream-style browse → card → practise → review loop works with Persian content |
| Broad initial coverage | Complete modules across several domains, including medicine and engineering, published as each is ready | Each module independently passes bilingual, specialist where applicable, media, and learning checks |
| Continuing expansion | Additional subcategories and depth bands across the full catalog | Coverage, change logs, credits, and progress compatibility update in the same release |
| Mature catalog | Cross-domain paths, more advanced modules, and richer diagrams | New modes remain compatible with the established Word Atlas interaction model and accessibility requirements |

The reference module is an integration fixture for the content pipeline, not a reduction of the comprehensive product scope. Broader work can proceed in parallel through bounded contributor queues while releases remain coherent and reviewable.

Required automated content checks include stable and unique IDs; valid taxonomy and path references; no published draft; required review evidence; valid credits and asset hashes; no missing assets for enabled exercises; sensible distractor eligibility; safe text and links; correct mixed-direction markup; release determinism; one public card and star per scoped lexical item; distinct headword and meaning counts; correct partial-state aggregation; and migration fixtures for renamed, split, and withdrawn senses. Reading checks additionally cover chapter availability, aligned paragraph and sentence references, exact target meanings, annotation anchors, question integrity, optional narration and checked timings, and bookmark migrations. Human checks should verify natural Persian, precise English meaning, useful examples, correct specialist concepts, clear visuals, pronunciation, and coherent reading translations and comprehension activities.

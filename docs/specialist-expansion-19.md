# Specialist vocabulary expansion 19

This editorial preview adds 24 distinct English entries: 12 in medicine and healthcare and 12 in engineering. The atlas now contains **690 words and 691 meanings across 32 categories**, with **266 words in each priority category**. Each needs 234 more published entries to reach its configured 500-entry target. Planning slots and private drafts are excluded.

Medicine adds outpatient, inpatient, referral, appointment, consent, discharge, admission, medical record, prescription, screening, triage and follow-up. The selected noun senses explain care journeys, communication and assessment. They distinguish an admitted stay from an outpatient visit, referral from scheduling, admission from discharge, a consent conversation from a signature, a chart from a medicine order, screening from diagnosis and triage from arrival order. Administrative definitions, prescribing rules and consent requirements vary. The pictures contain blank illustrative papers; they do not disclose actual patient information, issue prescriptions, establish legal permission or recommend care. Appearance and mobility do not determine admission status, screening eligibility or urgency.

Engineering adds stress, strain, tension, compression, shear, bending, torsion, deflection, vibration, frequency, amplitude and resonance. The selected mechanical senses separate force per area from relative deformation, axial and tangential load modes, twisting from bending, and tip displacement from applied load. Oscillation illustrations distinguish repetition rate, equilibrium-to-extreme amplitude and a finite response peak near a natural frequency. These are qualitative teaching diagrams. They provide no measured material properties, load limits, construction specifications or safety qualification. The strain silhouette exaggerates nonuniform deformation without asserting elastic recovery or actual failure.

All 24 entries have original English definitions, selected sense identities, IPA, approximate editorial CEFR, two meaningful examples and three collocations. Persian scaffolding and complete Brazilian Portuguese and Latin American Spanish overlays were authored together. The first example contains the complete English headword for practice. English remains the learning target in all three editions. Each complete set of language fields received a separate AI cross-review; independent human language and specialist review remain pending.

Every new picture was generated individually using the built-in image tool and the exact category brief: clean clinical cool-white/mint/pale-blue scenes for medicine, and precise slate/silver/amber technical renderings for engineering. Authors and second AI reviewers inspected actual full-size originals, final 512px WebPs and 120px thumbnails. Prompts, current hashes, semantic cues, category-profile hashes and actual findings are recorded with the published content. Review status remains explicitly AI-assisted and humanReviewed false.

Rejected candidates were kept privately. Corrections removed decorative plants and wall art from clinical scenes, changed a wide prescription composition into a square, removed a misleading shear reference, clarified torsion's material line and torque direction, removed rigid inner posts and inconsistent motion from the vibration example, and removed a contradictory actuator from resonance. The accepted oscillation graph shows a finite peak near the natural-frequency reference. Medical papers retain only essential checked headings or Rx symbols, with empty fields and no dose, diagnosis, signature or fabricated result.

Two older specialist pictures were also inspected at their actual 512px and 120px sizes. Patient still clearly shows a person receiving care in the clinical palette, and beam shows a connected horizontal I-section spanning two columns in the engineering palette. Both were retained. No older lexical record, sense identity, native overlay, audio identity or picture was replaced in this batch.

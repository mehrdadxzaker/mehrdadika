# Localization roadmap

Mehrdadika teaches English. Each interface locale supplies explanations, examples, discovery labels and learning guidance in the learner's language while preserving stable English lexical and sense identities.

## Current milestones

- **Persian (`fa`):** the full initial learning, review, reading and practice application.
- **Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`):** full shared application flows: onboarding/daily study, FSRS spaced review, illustrated Atlas and galaxy, reading/books, practice, settings and backups. All current starter content and taxonomy labels are translated; independent language and domain review remains open.
- **Latin American Spanish (`es-419`):** a first edition with the same core flows and translated starter scope as Portuguese. Regional terminology and native editorial review remain continuing work.
- **European Portuguese (`pt-PT`):** planned separately. Add reviewed terminology and an edition route before enabling its registry entry.

## Suggested languages after Spanish

This order is an editorial and implementation recommendation, not a measured market ranking. A committed native editor and qualified specialist reviewers can move a language ahead of a larger language without an editorial owner.

| Priority | Locale starting point | Reason and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Arabic, initially Modern Standard Arabic | Reuse tested RTL mechanics from Persian, with independent Arabic copy, typography checks and domain review. Arabic is among the largest non-English first-language groups taking the Duolingo English Test, a relevant but selective audience signal; dialect and country-specific terminology need explicit treatment. |
| 2 | French, with regional overrides | A useful edition across several regions. Recruit an editor who can distinguish broadly shared vocabulary from country-specific medical, legal and professional usage. |
| 3 | Indonesian (`id`) | A practical LTR pilot when a native editor is available. Its position here reflects implementation fit and reviewer capacity, not a demonstrated demand estimate. |
| 4 | Turkish (`tr`) | Another focused LTR expansion. Test locale-aware casing/search and independently review technical vocabulary. |

Hindi and Urdu should be separate projects with their own reviewers. Mandarin deserves consideration, but collaboration with the original Chinese Word Atlas may offer more value than immediately duplicating that edition.

The [Duolingo 2025 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/) identifies Mandarin, Spanish and Arabic among its largest non-English first-language groups taking the English Test. These are selected test takers, not the whole English-learning population. The [OIF 2026 report](https://www.francophonie.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/La_langue_francaise_dans_le_monde_Edition_2026.pdf) supplies geographic context for French; speaker reach alone is not product demand.

## Current starter scope

Portuguese and Spanish each include 72 words and 73 meanings, all 32 category and 348 subcategory labels, three short passages, one original three-chapter book, two paraphrase levels and three confusable-word groups. Shared English identities and media avoid duplicating learning meanings; edition-specific state keeps learner progress, sessions, reading and backups separate. Vocabulary and reading overlays compile against the exact published English release. The latest [word batch](word-expansion-2.md) starts law, arts, gaming and safety coverage while expanding cooking, healthcare and engineering.

Functional parity is separate from corpus size and editorial approval. Each edition remains an AI-assisted preview. Complete category labels do not imply that every planned lesson exists.

## Release approach

Start each language with a coherent module and honest preview labeling. Require native language review and appropriate medicine/engineering review before stable claims. Bind translations to their exact English source and revision, exclude drafts from learner bundles, preserve existing progress, and test LTR/RTL navigation, search, audio, dialogs and mobile layouts. Expand based on actual usage and contributor capacity.
